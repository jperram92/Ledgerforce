## Q-A. Which draft postings are compliant, which need changes, and which need an extra mechanism?

### Takeaway
The double-entry mechanics of GP-001 to GP-006 and GP-016 to GP-017 are consistent with AASB 15. The gaps are elsewhere:
- **No expected-credit-loss (ECL) allowance.** AASB 9 requires a lifetime ECL loss allowance on trade receivables at every reporting date. GP-007 is a direct write-off only, with no allowance account and no period-end adjustment.
- **Receivables recognised too early.** GP-014 and GP-016 debit AR when the invoice is raised. Under AASB 15 para 108 a receivable exists only once the right to payment is unconditional (Illustrative Example 38). A cancellable contract needs a different treatment.
- **No contract asset or unbilled revenue mechanism** for over-time services performed ahead of billing.
- **Customer credit balances sit as negative AR.** At reporting date they should be reclassified to a liability.
- **Credit-note reason codes are needed** so that price concessions (reduce revenue) are kept apart from credit losses (impairment expense).

### Cited Findings
- Para 108: a receivable is a right to consideration that is unconditional, meaning only the passage of time is required before payment is due. Para 106: if payment is received, or a receivable exists, before goods or services transfer, the entity presents a contract liability. A performance obligation can stay unsatisfied while the right to consideration is already unconditional. — [IFRS Community, contract assets and liabilities](https://ifrscommunity.com/knowledge-base/ifrs-15-contract-assets-and-contract-liabilities/) (search snippet); [IASB TRG paper ap7, presentation](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2014/october/trg-rev/rev-rec/ap7-presentation-contract.pdf)
- Para 105: unconditional rights are presented separately as receivables. An entity may present a receivable together with a contract asset, or a receivable together with a contract liability (Examples 39 and 40). — [IASB TRG ap7](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2014/october/trg-rev/rev-rec/ap7-presentation-contract.pdf) (search snippet)
- Example 38 (same under ASC 606):
  - Invoice issued **before a cancellation right lapses**: recognise neither a receivable nor a contract liability.
  - Contract **non-cancellable**: recognise the receivable and the contract liability when payment falls due, then cash settles the receivable.
  - Cash received under a cancellable contract: present it separately as a refund liability or similar.
  — [Deloitte DART Roadmap ch.14.2 Contract liabilities](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/revenue/asc606-10/roadmap-revenue-recognition/chapter-14-presentation/14-2-contract-liabilities); [Deloitte ch.14](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/revenue/asc606-10/roadmap-revenue-recognition/chapter-14-presentation?combine)
- BDO on advance billing: grossing up the balance sheet may not be appropriate. The contract liability and trade receivable should be shown net until the payment due date, which is when the receivable is recognised. The due date drives the timing, not the invoice date. — [BDO UK, "Will advance billing hurt your balance sheet?"](https://www.uk-tst2.bdo.global/en-gb/insights/business-edge/business-edge-2018/will-advance-billing-hurt-your-balance-sheet)
- Para 47: the transaction price excludes amounts collected on behalf of third parties, such as some sales taxes. GST collected for the government is not revenue. — [Disclosure Hub, determining the transaction price](https://disclosurehub.org/2014/08/18/accounting-for-revenue-determining-the-transaction-price/); [IASB staff paper ap7b, sales tax gross vs net](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2015/march/iasb/revenue-from-contracts-with-customers/ap7b-sales-tax-presentation-gross-versus-net.pdf)
- Para 55: a refund liability is recognised when consideration is received or receivable and the entity expects to refund some or all of it. It is measured at the amount the entity does not expect to keep and is updated at each reporting date. — [Disclosure Hub](https://disclosurehub.org/2024/01/30/more-issues-with-revenue-or-its-the-taxes-that-hurt/); [AASB standards node 92](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/node/92) (search listing)
- AASB 9 para 5.5.15: the simplified (lifetime ECL) approach is **mandatory** for trade receivables and contract assets from AASB 15 transactions without a significant financing component. — [IFRS Community, impairment](https://ifrscommunity.com/?p=606) (snippet); [IASB PIR feedback ap27c, May 2024](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2024/may/iasb/ap27c-feedback-analysis-other-matters.pdf)

That keeps GST (attributable at invoice issue) and the AR subledger consistent. This is an inference, not a cited practice.

## Q-B. Deposit invoices (GP-014: Dr AR / Cr customer prepayments, GST at invoice) — is a receivable before performance allowed, and how is the liability presented?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only when the right to the deposit is **unconditional**: the contract (or the deposit term) is non-cancellable and the deposit is due. Only the passage of time remains, and performance has not started (para 108, Example 38). Then Dr AR / Cr contract liability is correct. If the customer can still cancel without owing the deposit, AASB 15 recognises neither a receivable nor a contract liability, or presents them net, until payment is due or received. The credit belongs in a **contract liability**, measured **excluding GST**, and AASB 15 allows it to be labelled "customer deposits", "deferred revenue" or similar. **Crediting revenue on the deposit invoice is not compliant** (answers Q10: keep the liability).

### Cited Findings
- Para 108: a receivable is an unconditional right, with only the passage of time before payment is due. The performance obligation can remain unsatisfied while the right is unconditional. — [IFRS Community](https://ifrscommunity.com/knowledge-base/ifrs-15-contract-assets-and-contract-liabilities/) (snippet)
- Example 38: cancellable contract, invoice issued before cancellation lapses, so no receivable and no contract liability. Non-cancellable contract, so receivable plus contract liability when the amount is due. — [Deloitte DART 14.2](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/revenue/asc606-10/roadmap-revenue-recognition/chapter-14-presentation/14-2-contract-liabilities); [Selden Fox](https://seldenfox.com/?p=3015) (search listing)
- Advance funds under a cancellable term are presented separately from any contract liability, as a refund liability or similar. — [Deloitte DART 4.6, consideration received when contract criteria not met](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/revenue/asc606-10/roadmap-revenue-recognition/chapter-4-step-1-identify-contract/4-6-consideration-received-when-criteria)
- Net presentation until the payment due date, which is when the receivable is recognised. — [BDO UK](https://www.uk-tst2.bdo.global/en-gb/insights/business-edge/business-edge-2018/will-advance-billing-hurt-your-balance-sheet)
- Para 109: AASB 15 uses the terms "contract asset" and "contract liability" but does not prohibit alternative descriptions. If an alternative is used for a contract asset, the entity must give enough information to tell receivables and contract assets apart. — **unverified, from prior knowledge** (the paragraph is widely known; its text was not retrieved).
- GST is excluded from the transaction price (para 47), so the contract liability is the ex-GST amount. — [Disclosure Hub](https://disclosurehub.org/2014/08/18/accounting-for-revenue-determining-the-transaction-price/)

### Inferences
- GP-014 as drafted (Cr CUSTOMER_PREPAYMENTS 3,000.00 ex GST, GST 300.00) **measures the liability correctly**. The open issue is **when AR is recognised**.
- The trigger "Opportunity Closed Won" is a signed contract, but SMB deposit terms are often cancellable or refundable. Recommended mechanism:
  - (a) A deposit-invoice template flag `deposit_non_refundable` / `contract_non_cancellable`.
  - (b) If true: GP-014 stands.
  - (c) If false: GP-014 can still post. Under accruals GST is attributable once the tax invoice is issued, so the GST line must post regardless (tax analysis belongs to the other researcher). The **balance-sheet report nets** unpaid deposit AR against the related contract liability. A stricter alternative: issue the deposit as a payment request (not a tax invoice) and post only on receipt via GP-012 Option B.
- Naming: keep the user-facing names "Customer deposits" (2400) and "Deferred revenue" (2410), but tag both with a reporting class `contract_liability` so that Tier 2 statements aggregate them as contract liabilities (AASB 1060 still requires contract balances disclosure: **unverified**). The system role names CUSTOMER_PREPAYMENTS and DEFERRED_REVENUE can stay. A combined "contract liabilities" line in the balance-sheet template is the practical answer.
- A deposit refunded on cancellation: Dr contract liability (or refund liability) / Cr bank, plus a GST adjustment note. No revenue is involved.
- A non-refundable deposit forfeited on cancellation: revenue is recognised when the obligation is extinguished. That falls under the breakage / customer's unexercised rights guidance (paras B44 to B47, **unverified**), and the product owner should decide it as a Draft golden posting.

## Q-C. Bad debts: is direct write-off acceptable for small entities, and what minimal ECL approach is practical?

### Takeaway
Tier 2 (AASB 1060) only reduces **disclosures**. AASB 9 recognition and measurement, including the impairment model, applies in full. Direct write-off alone does not comply with AASB 9 unless the resulting difference is immaterial. AASB 9 mandates a lifetime ECL allowance for trade receivables (simplified approach, para 5.5.15) and permits a **provision matrix** (B5.5.35) based on ageing buckets × historical loss rates, adjusted for current and forward-looking conditions. Separately, para 5.4.4 **requires** a write-off when there is no reasonable expectation of recovery. Most small proprietary companies have no statutory obligation to prepare AASB financial statements unless shareholders or ASIC direct, or they are foreign-controlled. For them, direct write-off is a tax and management convenience, not AASB compliance, so LedgerForce should offer the allowance mechanism and default it on.

### Cited Findings
- AASB 1060 does not change Tier 2 recognition and measurement requirements; they are the same as Tier 1. — [AASB 1060 explanatory statement, legislation.gov.au F2020L00288](https://legislation.gov.au/F2020L00288/asmade/2020-03-23/es/original/word); [William Buck](https://williambuck.com/nfp-financial-reporting-insights/)
- From 1 July 2021, for-profit entities required by legislation to comply with AAS can no longer prepare special purpose statements and must apply full recognition and measurement. — [William Buck](https://williambuck.com/?p=12824) (search snippet)
- Domestic small proprietary companies are not required to prepare reports by default. Shareholders holding ≥5% of votes may direct (s293), and ASIC may request (s294). Foreign-controlled small proprietary companies must comply with Part 2M, subject to relief under ASIC Instrument 2017/204. — [ASIC, small proprietary companies](https://asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-reporting-and-audit/preparers-of-financial-reports/small-proprietary-companies); [ASIC, shareholder-requested reports](https://asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-reporting-and-audit/preparers-of-financial-reports/small-proprietary-companies/small-proprietary-companies-not-controlled-by-a-foreign-company-and-are-not-disclosing-entities-where-shareholders-request-a-financial-report/); [RSM](https://www.rsm.global/australia/insights/ifrs-news/reporting-obligations-australian-proprietary-companies)
- 5.5.15: simplified approach mandatory for trade receivables or contract assets without a significant financing component; optional where there is one. The provision matrix is a practical expedient (B5.5.35), not mandatory. It is built from historical loss experience adjusted for current conditions and forecasts, grouped by ageing and by customer segment where loss patterns differ. Omitting the forward-looking adjustment is a common error (5.5.17(c)). — [IFRS Community](https://ifrscommunity.com/?p=606) (snippet); [PwC Switzerland, Provision Matrix Practical Guide](https://www.pwc.ch/en/publications/2020/IFRS%209%20-%20Impairment%20-%20Provision%20Matrix%20-%20Practical%20Guide.pdf); [MNP guide, impairment of trade receivables](https://www.mnp.ca/-/media/files/mnp/pdf/service/assurance-and-accounting/financial-reporting-library/2019-07-ifrs-9-guide-impairment-of-trade-receivables.pdf); [RSM, provision matrix practical expedient](https://www.rsm.global/node/401); [Mondaq, provision matrix](https://www.mondaq.com/Article/819718)
- 5.4.4: an entity **shall** directly reduce the gross carrying amount when it has no reasonable expectation of recovering the asset in full or in part. A write-off is a derecognition event (B3.2.16(r)). IFRS 9 does not list the situations that meet the test; it is a judgement. Enforcement can continue after write-off. — [IASB staff paper ap27c, April 2024](https://cdn.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2024/april/iasb/ap27c-feedback-analysis-application-ifrs9-impairment-requirements.pdf); [EBA Q&A 2017_3082](https://eba.europa.eu/single-rule-book-qa/qna/view/publicId/2017_3082); [BCRA](https://www.bcra.gob.ar/en/removal-of-irrecoverable-receivables-from-the-asset/)
- Impairment losses, including reversals and impairment gains, are presented as a separate line under IAS 1 para 82(ba) (AASB 101). Practice includes recoveries of written-off amounts within the impairment line. — [BDO India newsletter (Ind AS equivalent)](https://www.bdo.in/getmedia/ef670680-3991-4b30-978d-ac0f4306ec00/BDO-India-Newsletter_March-2025.pdf); practice example [SEC filing R79](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1073404/000110465920041231/R79.htm). Textbook (non-IFRS) sources instead credit "bad debts expense" on recovery. — [pastpaperhero ACCA FA2](https://www.pastpaperhero.com/resources/acca-fa2-receivables-adjustments-recoveries-of-bad-debts)
- AASB 18 (which replaces AASB 101 presentation) is effective for for-profit entities for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027. AASB 2024-2 (classification and measurement amendments to AASB 9 and AASB 7) applies from 1 January 2026. AASB 2025-2 adds Tier 2 disclosures for it, also from 1 January 2026. None of these changes the trade-receivable ECL model. — [AASB news, AASB 2024-2](https://aasb.gov.au/news/aasb-2024-2-amendments-to-australian-accounting-standards-classification-and-measurement-of-financial-instruments/); [AASB standards listing](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/accounting-standards?page=0); [Grant Thornton TA 2026-1](https://www.grantthornton.com.au/insights/technical-alerts/ta-2026-1-whats-new-for-31-december-2025-reporting/); [Accru, AASB 2025-2](https://accru.com/?p=20931). (The claim that these amendments do not affect the simplified approach is an inference; AASB 2024-2 concerns classification, SPPI and e-payment derecognition per prior knowledge, **unverified**.)

### Inferences — minimal practical ECL design for SMB software
1. **New system role `AR_ECL_ALLOWANCE`** (contra-asset under receivables) and setting `ar.impairment_expense_account`. The latter can be the existing 6300 "Bad debts", renamed "Impairment of receivables (credit losses)".
2. **Period-end provision-matrix job** (closing checklist). Ageing buckets (current, 1–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days past due) × loss rate per bucket. Rates are tenant-set, defaulting from the tenant's own write-off history over the last N years where available. There is an override field for a forward-looking adjustment with a mandatory note. Specific (individually assessed) debtors can carry a 100% rate. The job posts a **single adjusting journal for the delta**, Dr impairment expense / Cr allowance (or the reverse when the allowance falls), net of GST. Approval is required. Contract assets should be included once they exist.
3. **Write-off (GP-007 superseded)**: Dr AR_ECL_ALLOWANCE (ex-GST amount) / Cr AR_CONTROL. Any shortfall beyond the allowance goes Dr impairment expense. The ECL measured on GST-inclusive receivables should cover the GST portion only where the GST is not recoverable through a decreasing adjustment; that refinement is a judgement for the product owner.
4. **Decouple the GST bad-debt adjustment** from the accounting write-off. Under the GST Act (s21-5, **unverified from prior knowledge**; another researcher covers GST) the decreasing adjustment needs the debt to be written off as bad **or** overdue 12 months or more. That may not coincide with the AASB 9 write-off date (5.4.4 can be earlier or later). So the GST adjustment line (Dr GST / Cr allowance or AR) should be its own tax-event posting.
5. **Recovery**: Dr bank / Cr impairment expense (an impairment gain in the same AASB 101 82(ba) line). Alternatively reinstate the receivable (Dr AR / Cr impairment) and then receipt it. GST increasing adjustment at 1A per the other researcher.
6. **Small tenants** may set the matrix to "specific provisions only" and leave the default rates at 0%. Document this as a materiality judgement; the setting itself does not establish compliance.
7. Credit notes issued **because the customer cannot pay** must go through write-off or impairment, not revenue. Credit notes issued as a commercial price concession reduce revenue. The IASB itself acknowledges IFRS 15 has no bright-line test and practice diverges. — [IASB 2013 staff paper ap7a collectability](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2013/july/iasb/revenue-recognition/ap7a-collectability.pdf); [IASB PIR ap6a, applying IFRS 15 with IFRS 9 (2024)](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2024/april/iasb/ap6a-ifrs15-pir-applying-ifrs-15-with-ifrs-9.pdf); [RevenueHub, price concessions](https://www.revenuehub.org/price-concessions/). So: a **reason code** on credit notes, with the "credit loss" reason routed to the write-off posting.
8. An **implicit price concession expected at inception** (the seller habitually accepts less) reduces the transaction price under the variable-consideration constraint (paras 50 to 54, **unverified**). This is rare for SMBs and can be left out of scope, with a note.

## Q-D. Stripe fees, significant financing, rounding, and reversals/voids

### Takeaway
- **Stripe fees**: an operating expense. Revenue stays gross because the merchant is principal for its own goods and services and Stripe is a supplier, not a customer. GP-003 and GP-004 comply.
- **Significant financing**: the para 63 practical expedient (≤12 months between transfer and payment, assessed at inception) covers normal SMB terms, annual prepaid subscriptions and deposits. No financing posting is needed. A guard is needed for contracts longer than 12 months.
- **Rounding**: GP-018 is immaterial and acceptable.
- **Voids**: a void or reversal in the current open period is ordinary bookkeeping. Correcting a **prior-period error** under AASB 108 is a reporting-layer restatement, not a back-dated ledger entry.

### Cited Findings
- There is no explicit IFRS guidance on credit-card fees. The relevant test is principal versus agent: who controls the good or service before transfer (B34 onward). — [KPMG Defining Issues 15-31](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2015/07/Defining-Issues-O-1507-31.pdf); [Deloitte, revenue recognition in payments](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/us/Documents/audit/us-revenue-recognition-payments-pov.pdf); [HubiFi, principal vs agent](https://www.hubifi.com/blog/ifrs-15-agent-vs-principal)
- Merchant fees are presented as an operating expense, not deducted from revenue. Consideration payable reduces revenue only when it is paid to a **customer** (para 70), and Stripe is not the customer. **Unverified, from prior knowledge**: no retrieved source states this directly. The KPMG and Deloitte items above only frame the question.
- Para 63 expedient: no financing adjustment if at inception the expected period between transfer and payment is ≤ 1 year. It applies to advance payments as well as deferred payments. — [BDO Australia, significant financing component](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/content/accounting-news/accounting-news-march-2019/contract-with-a-customer); [Deloitte DART 6.4](https://dart.deloitte.com/USDART/home/codification/revenue/asc606-10/roadmap-revenue-recognition/chapter-6-step-3-determine-transaction/6-4-significant-financing-component); [BDO NZ](https://www.bdo.nz/en-nz/blogs/financial-reporting-insights/accounting-for-a-significant-financing-component-in-a-contract-with-a-customer)
- The simplified approach is mandatory only for receivables without a significant financing component (5.5.15). Where one exists it is an option, so the expedient also keeps the ECL design simple. — [IFRS Community](https://ifrscommunity.com/?p=606) (snippet)
- SaaS access is a stand-ready obligation satisfied over time, and straight-line recognition is the usual measure of progress. — [Deloitte tech alert (ASC 606)](https://www2.deloitte.com/content/dam/Deloitte/us/Documents/audit/us-audit-tech-alert-accounting-for-cloud-basedor-hosted-software-arrangements-with-variable-consideration.pdf); [PwC, IFRS 15 for the software industry](https://pwc.com/gx/en/services/audit-assurance/assets/ifrs-15-for-the-software-industry-in-brief.pdf); [Grant Thornton NZ, software and cloud services](https://www.grantthornton.co.nz/globalassets/1.-member-firms/new-zealand/pdfs/ifrs-guides/software-and-cloud-services-industry.pdf)
- AASB 108 (brief, **unverified, from prior knowledge**): prior-period errors are corrected retrospectively by restating comparatives (paras 42 to 49). Errors found in the current period before the statements are authorised are corrected in the current period. Materiality governs. Another researcher covers this in depth.

### Inferences
- **GP-003 / GP-004**: compliant. Q1 (fee at charge versus at payout) is a cut-off question, not an AASB 15 one. At a month end between charge and payout, the fee incurred should be accrued if material. Recognising the fee at charge time, when Stripe reports it, gives cleaner cut-off. The Stripe clearing balance is a **receivable from the processor**, a financial asset within AASB 9 with negligible ECL. Present it under receivables or other financial assets, not as cash.
- **Principal versus agent** matters for tenants who resell third-party products (marketplaces, reimbursable expenses, product profile "Reimbursable expense" in §4.4). Agent revenue is the net commission. LedgerForce should let a product profile be flagged `agent` so the posting profile credits a payable to the principal plus commission revenue. That is a future Draft golden posting, not MVP.
- **Financing guard**: when a billing plan, milestone schedule or deposit implies more than 12 months between payment and performance, the preview should warn that a significant financing component may exist and needs accountant review. No automatic interest posting.
- **GP-018 rounding**: cent-level differences are immaterial. Posting them to ROUNDING (8900) or adjusting the last revenue line are both acceptable under AASB 15; Q8 is a GST and presentation choice. Map ROUNDING to the revenue (or other income) reporting class so GST rounding does not distort expense lines. This is an inference.
- **GP-019 void**: a reversal dated in the first open period is right for the ledger. Blocking a void once allocations exist (Q11) is sound. If an invoice in a **closed, already-reported** period was wrong and material, AASB 108 needs a restatement of comparatives in the financial statements, so the reporting layer needs a "prior-period adjustment" flag on the reversal entry. Locked periods are never reopened silently.
