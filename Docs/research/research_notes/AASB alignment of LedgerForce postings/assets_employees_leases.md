## Q1. Do GP-008 (bill with capital asset), GP-009 (supplier payment), GP-010 (payroll journal) and GP-011 (net pay) comply, and what is missing?

### Takeaway
The four journals are individually correct double-entry and consistent with AASB 116 initial recognition, AASB 119 short-term benefits and liability derecognition on payment; none breaches a standard. What fails AASB compliance is what happens *after* them: no depreciation of the capitalised laptop (fixed assets register is Phase 2 while capitalisation is MVP), no annual-leave/LSL provisions, no accrued-expense/prepayment mechanism with auto-reversal, and the super-payable design assumes quarterly settlement whereas Payday Super (law, in force for paydays from 1 July 2026 — i.e. before GP-010's 2027-07-14 pay run) requires super to reach the fund within 7 business days of each payday.

### Cited Findings

**Superannuation guarantee rate and Payday Super (status as found, Nov 2026)**
- SG rate rose to 12% on 1 July 2025; ATO: "The super guarantee (SG) rate will increase to 12% on 1 July 2025"; it applies to salary and wages *paid* on/after 1 July 2025 even if part of the pay period was earlier; 12% is the last scheduled increase — [ATO small business newsroom](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/small-business-newsroom/the-final-sg-rate-increase-is-coming-on-1-july); [AustralianSuper](https://www.australiansuper.com/superannuation/superannuation-articles/2024/07/understanding-the-superannuation-guarantee). Payday Super does not change the 12% rate — [digit.business](https://digit.business/insights/people-payroll/super-guarantee-rate-2025-26-australia).
- Payday Super legislation passed (ATO media release: legislation "has now passed and will take effect from 1 July 2026") — [ATO media centre](https://www.ato.gov.au/media-centre/ato-calls-on-employers-to-prepare-for-payday-super). Act named as Treasury Laws Amendment (Payday Superannuation) Act 2025, passed 6 Nov 2025 — [Lawpath](https://lawpath.com.au/blog/payday-super); [Capricorn Enterprise "Payday Super is now Law"](https://capricornenterprise.com.au/2025/11/payday-super-is-now-law/). Treasury Laws Amendment (Payday Superannuation) Regulations 2026 made 19 Feb 2026 — per search summary of [Lawpath 1 July 2026 changes](https://lawpath.com.au/blog/17-changes-hitting-australian-businesses-on-1-july-2026/2) (secondary; not verified on legislation.gov.au).
- Rule: applies to paydays on/after 1 July 2026; the "usual period" is **seven business days after payday**; an "extended period" applies to a new employee's first payday and the next payday after an employee chooses a new fund — [ATO making super payments](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/super-for-employers/payday-super/paying-super-on-payday/making-super-payments) (via search text). Extended period described as **20 business days** by [Mercer Super](https://www.mercersuper.com.au/employers/learn/payday-super-changes); CFS says "20 days" — [CFS](https://www.cfs.com.au/employer/paydaysuper) (minor conflict; ATO wording not directly read).
- New SG base "qualifying earnings" (QE) = ordinary time earnings + commissions (plus other items); SG = 12% × QE for each "QE day" (the payday); SG charge assessed per QE day; the new SGC is tax deductible but post-assessment interest is not — [ATO: the new super guarantee charge](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/super-for-employers/payday-super/missed-or-late-payday-super-payments/the-new-super-guarantee-charge); [Mercer](https://www.mercersuper.com.au/employers/learn/payday-super-changes).
- Small Business Superannuation Clearing House closed 1 July 2026 (one secondary source) — [Lawpath](https://lawpath.com.au/blog/payday-super). ATO Practical Compliance Guideline for 1 Jul 2026–30 Jun 2027: ATO will not review "low risk" employers — per search summary of [ATO media centre](https://www.ato.gov.au/media-centre/ato-calls-on-employers-to-prepare-for-payday-super) / [RSM](https://www.rsm.global/australia/insights/payday-super-legislation-reforms) (PCG number not found).
- Fair Work Ombudsman has a news item "Payday Super: New rules starting 1 July 2026" — [Fair Work](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/newsroom/news/payday-super-new-rules-starting-1-july-2026) (title only; fetch blocked).

**AASB 116 (PPE) — relevant to GP-008**
- Each part of an item of PPE with a cost significant in relation to the total cost shall be depreciated separately (para 43); parts with the same useful life and method may be grouped — [Vic DTF guidance on component depreciation](https://dtf.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-10/Guidance-on-depreciation-of-building-components-under-FRD-103.docx); [BDO common errors](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/content/accounting-news/accounting-news-july-2018/more-common-errors).
- Depreciation method guidance sits in paras 60–62; the method must reflect the pattern in which future economic benefits are consumed; AASB 2014-4 added para 62A (revenue-based depreciation presumed inappropriate) — [AASB 2014-4](https://www.aasb.gov.au/admin/file/content105/c9/AASB2014-4_08-14.pdf); [AASB 116 compiled (2015)](https://legislation.gov.au/F2015L01572/asmade/2015-09-30/text/original/pdf).
- Prior knowledge (unverified — from prior knowledge): recognition criteria para 7 (probable future economic benefits + cost reliably measurable); para 9 permits aggregating individually insignificant items (basis for a capitalisation threshold policy); cost includes purchase price net of recoverable taxes (so GST claimable as input tax credit is excluded — consistent with GP-008 debiting 1510 at $2,000 ex GST); para 50 depreciable amount allocated systematically over useful life; para 51 residual value and useful life reviewed at least each financial year end; para 55 depreciation begins when available for use; para 62 lists straight-line, diminishing balance and units of production; paras 67–72 derecognition on disposal, gain/loss = net disposal proceeds − carrying amount, not classified as revenue.

**ATO tax depreciation (tax only, not AASB)**
- Instant asset write-off $20,000 per asset for small business entities (aggregated turnover < $10m), extended to 30 June 2026 and law — [ATO $20,000 IAWO](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/new-legislation/in-detail/businesses/20000-dollars-instant-asset-write-off). 2026–27 Budget (12 May 2026) proposed making $20,000 permanent from 1 July 2026; **status conflicting**: Accountants Daily says law — [Accountants Daily](https://www.accountantsdaily.com.au/tax-compliance/22847-ato-issues-update-on-loss-carry-back-tax-offset-instant-asset-write-off-incentives); Aspire says passed both houses 19 Aug 2026 but awaiting Royal Assent — [Aspire](https://aspireapp.com/en-au/blog/instant-asset-write-off-rules); [Sparkreceipt](https://sparkreceipt.com/en-au/blog/instant-asset-write-off/) and [Austax](https://austax.tools/tax-insights/instant-asset-write-off-2025-26/) say not yet legislated (as of mid-2026).
- Simplified depreciation small business pool: 15% in allocation year, 30% each later year; pool written off when balance falls below the IAWO limit; "lock out" rule suspended 12 May 2015–30 June 2026 — [ATO simpler depreciation for small business](https://www.ato.gov.au/business/depreciation-and-capital-expenses-and-allowances/simpler-depreciation-for-small-business/); [Lawpath pool rule](https://lawpath.com.au/blog/how-the-small-business-pool-deduction-rule-works).

**AASB 119 (employee benefits) — relevant to GP-010/011**
- Short-term benefits are those expected to be settled wholly before 12 months after the end of the reporting period in which service is rendered; other long-term benefits include long service leave; short-term measured undiscounted (nominal), other long-term at present value using high-quality corporate bond rates; shorthand measurement techniques permitted; annual leave can be short- or long-term depending on expected settlement — [NSW Treasury TPG24-23](https://www.treasury.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-07/tpg24-23-accounting-for-long-service-leave.pdf); [NSW TC21-03](https://treasury.nsw.gov.au/index.php/documents/tc21-03-accounting-long-service-leave-and-annual-leave); [Nexia](https://nexia.com.au/node/199); [AASB 119 compiled](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/node/248). AU corporate bond market for LSL discounting (G100 rates) — [BDO](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/blogs/corporate-reporting/december-2022/corporate-bond-rates-for-long-service-leave-calculations-available-for-purchase-from-the-g100).
- Prior knowledge (unverified — from prior knowledge): AASB 119 para 11 — short-term benefits recognised as liability (accrued expense) and expense when service rendered; paras 13–16 — accumulating paid absences (annual leave) recognised as service is rendered that increases entitlement; defined contribution (SG) — para 51: contribution payable recognised as liability (accrued expense) after deducting amounts already paid. NES: 4 weeks' paid annual leave (accumulating), 10 days personal/carer's leave; LSL is State/Territory legislation (typically after 7–10 years) — unverified, not fetched.

**AASB 137 / accruals**
- Provisions distinguished from trade payables and accruals by uncertainty about timing or amount (IAS 37 para 11); accruals are amounts for goods/services received but not paid, invoiced or formally agreed, including amounts due to employees (e.g. accrued vacation pay); accruals reported within trade and other payables, provisions separately — [IAS 37 text, FAR Online](https://www.faronline.se/dokument/ifrs-english/ias/ias0037_eng/) (AASB 137 is the AU equivalent; paragraph numbering assumed identical — unverified).
- AASB 101 para 27: entity prepares financial statements, except cash flow information, using the accrual basis — unverified — from prior knowledge (search didn't surface text). Prior knowledge: AASB 18 (IFRS 18 equivalent) replaces AASB 101 for for-profit entities for periods beginning on/after 1 Jan 2027, with the accrual-basis requirement relocated to AASB 108 — unverified.

### Inferences
- **GP-008 compliant at initial recognition.** Capitalising a $2,000 laptop to "Computer equipment" ex GST meets AASB 116 cost; GST-CAP tax code and G10 are tax-correct. But the chart has no accumulated-depreciation or depreciation-expense account, and design §9.1 puts the "fixed assets register" in Phase 2 while capitalisation happens in MVP. The MVP pilot month-end (UC-10) would therefore overstate assets/understate expense unless a manual/recurring depreciation journal is posted. Missing: `system:ACCUM_DEPRECIATION` per asset class (e.g. 1519), `DEPRECIATION_EXPENSE`, a capitalisation-threshold company setting (policy, AASB 116 para 9 aggregation), and a disposal golden (proceeds, GST on sale at 1A/G1, gain/loss on disposal, derecognise cost and accumulated depreciation).
- **Tax vs book divergence.** If the user's accountant uses the $20,000 IAWO, the laptop is fully deductible for tax but must still be depreciated over its useful life in AASB statements. LedgerForce should keep book depreciation only in the GL; tax depreciation (IAWO / 15%/30% pool) belongs in a tax-reporting layer or the tax agent's software, not the ledger. Do not offer "expense under IAWO limit" as a default book policy without labelling it as an entity materiality policy.
- **GP-009 compliant.** Derecognising AP on bank confirmation (not on release) is consistent with derecognising a financial liability when extinguished (AASB 9 — outside this note's scope). Q6 granularity is presentation/reconciliation, not an AASB matter.
- **GP-010 compliant in structure** (Dr wages expense, Dr super expense; Cr PAYGW, Cr super payable, Cr wages payable = the standard AU payroll journal). Issues:
  1. **Super base**: super $1,200 = 12% × gross $10,000 only because the scenario assumes all gross is QE/OTE. The engine must take the SG amount from the payroll provider, not compute 12% × gross (overtime is generally not OTE; salary sacrifice changes the base). Suggest the golden note says so.
  2. **Payday Super timing**: the 2027-07-14 pay run is under Payday Super, so SUPER_PAYABLE must be cleared to funds within 7 business days (20 for new employees/new fund). UC-07 currently creates only a net-pay ABA batch; it needs a **super payment** golden (Dr SUPER_PAYABLE / Cr bank, posted on confirmation, likely via a commercial clearing house since SBSCH closed) and a due-date/ageing on SUPER_PAYABLE per pay run (a missed deadline creates SGC exposure, which is a non-deductible-interest liability — a separate golden for SGC/interest expense may be needed).
  3. **PAYGW** is remitted on the BAS/IAS cycle (W2), so its liability legitimately ages longer — keep it separate from super.
  4. **Q5 PAYROLL_CLEARING**: where the provider (or its clearing service) pays net pay and super directly, post Cr PAYROLL_CLEARING instead of WAGES_PAYABLE/SUPER_PAYABLE and clear it against the single bank debit; this is a presentation choice, no AASB impact.
  5. **Not in scope of goldens but required for AASB 119**: annual leave and LSL provisions (and on-costs such as super on leave where payable, payroll tax and workers comp — on-cost treatment unverified), state payroll tax and workers' compensation accruals. GP-010 records only the cash pay run.
- **GP-011 compliant.** Net-pay ABA batch clears WAGES_PAYABLE on confirmation.
- **Period-end gap.** Design §3.7 already has auto-reversing revaluation entries; the same "post and auto-reverse on day 1 of next period" capability is needed for accrued expenses (goods/services received, bill not yet received), accrued wages (pay period straddling month end) and accrued super. UC-10 lists "accruals" as a checklist step but no golden or mechanism exists. Prepayments (Dr prepaid expense asset / Cr AP, then monthly release to expense) are also absent from §3.8.

## Q2. What minimum fixed-asset, accrual and employee-provision capabilities should an AU SMB ledger provide for AASB-compliant Tier 2 statements? (incl. AASB 138, 16, 102)

### Takeaway
Tier 2 (AASB 1060 Simplified Disclosures) cuts disclosures only — all AASB recognition and measurement applies — so even a services-first SMB ledger needs: a basic fixed-asset register with straight-line/diminishing-balance depreciation runs and disposals; auto-reversing accrual and prepayment journals; annual-leave and LSL provisions (imported or calculated); SaaS costs expensed by default with an override to capitalise only when an intangible asset is controlled; lease exemptions (short-term/low-value) as default with a manual ROU journal path; inventory optional (not needed for services MVP).

### Cited Findings
- **Tier 2**: AASB 1060 contains Tier 2 presentation/disclosure for periods beginning on/after 1 July 2021; Tier 2 GPFS "requires entities to apply all recognition and measurement requirements of Australian Accounting Standards"; AASB 2020-2 removed self-assessed SPFS for certain for-profit entities (e.g. unlisted public companies, small proprietary companies controlled by a foreign company, crowd-sourced funding companies, AFSL holders) — [KPMG Farewell SPFS](https://kpmg.com/au/en/insights/financial-reporting/farewell-spfs-welcome-simplified-disclosures.html); [Grant Thornton alert](https://www.grantthornton.com.au/globalassets/1.-member-firms/australian-website/technical-publications/local-technical--financial-alerts/gtal_2020_ta-2020-06-removal-of-spfs-and-the-introduction-of-simplified-disclosure-framework2888895.3.pdf); [Pitcher](https://www.pitcher.com.au/insights/what-does-the-change-in-the-reporting-entity-concept-mean-for-special-purpose-financial-reporting/); [BDO](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/content/accounting-news/accounting-news-april-2020/special-purpose-financial-statements). Not confirmed: whether ordinary small proprietary companies outside those groups may still use SPFS (prior knowledge: most small proprietary companies have no Corporations Act obligation to lodge financial statements at all — unverified).
- **AASB 138 / SaaS**: IFRIC March 2019 agenda decision — a contract giving only a right to access supplier-hosted software in the future is a service contract, not an intangible asset or lease — [IAS Plus summary](https://www.iasplus.com/en/meeting-notes/ifrs-ic/2019/march/ias-38); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/insights/audit/cloud-computing-arrangements.html). IFRIC March 2021 agenda decision (ratified by IASB April 2021) on configuration/customisation costs: if no intangible asset is recognised, costs are expensed when the services are received; if configuration/customisation by the supplier is not distinct from SaaS access, expense over the SaaS term (i.e. prepayment); may require reversing previously capitalised costs — [IFRS IC update March 2021](https://www.ifrs.org/content/ifrs/home/news-and-events/updates/ifric/2021/ifric-update-march-2021.html); [BDO AU](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/content/accounting-news/accounting-news-may-2021/saas-implementation-costs); [RSM](https://www.rsm.global/sites/default/files/media/Ideas%20and%20insight/IFRS/rsm_insight_accounting_for_cloud_based_software.pdf); [QAO](https://qao.qld.gov.au/blog/configuration-or-customisation-costs-cloud-computing-arrangement). Deloitte notes the answer is at odds with many preparers' perceptions — [Deloitte](https://www2.deloitte.com/ie/en/pages/audit/articles/accounting-for-software-as-a-service-cost-a-cloudy-topic.html). (Note: one search summary says the "distinct → prepayment" split; the prepayment-over-term limb is from prior knowledge of the decision — unverified.)
- **AASB 16**: two optional recognition exemptions — short-term lease (≤12 months at commencement, no purchase option; elected by class of underlying asset) and low-value asset (assessed per lease on value when new; Basis for Conclusions refers to US$5,000 or less; not available if subleased); exemptions don't remove disclosure requirements; no size-based SME exemption — [NSW Treasury AASB 16 guidance](https://www.treasury.nsw.gov.au/documents/guidance-aasb-16-leases-new-lease-standards); [House of Control](https://www.houseofcontrol.com/blog/ifrs-16-exemptions-short-term-and-low-value); [Commonwealth Finance RMG 110](https://www.finance.gov.au/government/managing-commonwealth-resources/accounting-leases-rmg-110/identifying-lease) (public sector uses AUD 10,000 — not binding on private SMEs).
- **AASB 102**: not researched by search; prior knowledge (unverified): inventories measured at lower of cost and NRV, cost formulas FIFO or weighted average (LIFO prohibited); applies to goods held for sale and, for service providers, materials/supplies consumed in rendering services; service providers' work in progress costs now mostly fall under AASB 15 contract costs rather than AASB 102.

### Inferences — minimum capability set (proposed, for golden/ADR discussion)
1. **Fixed assets (pull forward a "lite" register into MVP or Phase 1, not Phase 2):** asset record created from a bill line coded to a fixed-asset account (GP-008 trigger); fields: class, cost (ex recoverable GST), in-service date, useful life, residual value, method (straight-line, diminishing balance; units-of-production optional), parent/component link (componentisation optional for SMB); monthly depreciation run posting Dr depreciation expense / Cr accumulated depreciation (through the posting engine, idempotent per asset+period); annual review of life/residual (AASB 116 para 51 — unverified number) as a close-checklist item; disposal posting with gain/loss and GST on sale; capitalisation threshold setting; optional tax-depreciation shadow book (IAWO / small business pool) kept off the GL. New goldens needed: depreciation run, disposal with gain, disposal with loss/scrap.
2. **Accruals & prepayments:** "auto-reversing journal" flag on manual and system journals (reversal dated first day of next period, linked via `reversal_of_id`, consistent with immutability); accrued-expense template (Dr expense / Cr ACCRUED_EXPENSES liability, new system role), prepayment schedules (Dr PREPAYMENTS asset on bill, straight-line release over service term) — reuses the Phase 2 deferred-revenue schedule engine mirrored to the expense side; covers SaaS-implementation-cost prepayments under the 2021 IFRIC decision. Accruals sit in trade and other payables; provisions (AASB 137) need a separate account subtype and should be rare for SMBs (make-good, warranty).
3. **Employee provisions (Phase 1 with Payroll Bridge):** import leave balances (hours × rate) from the payroll provider each period and post the movement: Dr leave expense / Cr provision for annual leave (current) and provision for LSL (split current/non-current); LSL at present value via shorthand (probability-of-vesting × discount factor table) entered as a period-end journal or parameterised calculator; accrued wages for pay periods straddling period end (auto-reversing). New system roles: ANNUAL_LEAVE_PROVISION, LSL_PROVISION (current/non-current), ACCRUED_WAGES; optional PAYROLL_TAX_PAYABLE.
4. **Super under Payday Super:** per-pay-run SUPER_PAYABLE open item with a due date = payday + 7 business days (20 for flagged new employees/fund changes); super payment batch posting on confirmation; "overdue super" alert on payables summary (UC-07); SGC/interest expense account.
5. **Leases:** default policy = elect short-term and low-value exemptions (straight-line expense via bills/recurring journals); for any other lease, support a manual ROU asset / lease liability opening journal plus an amortisation schedule (could reuse the fixed-asset depreciation engine for the ROU asset and a simple effective-interest schedule for the liability). A full lease module is not MVP-critical for a services SMB but the GL must allow it.
6. **SaaS/software:** default bill coding of subscriptions to expense (or prepayment if annual in advance); capitalised software (account subtype "intangible", amortised through the same depreciation engine) only where the entity controls the software (on-premise licence or own development).
7. **Inventory:** not needed for the services-first MVP; GL chart template "AU trade" will eventually need perpetual or periodic inventory (FIFO/WAC, lower of cost and NRV); a periodic stock-count adjustment journal suffices initially.
