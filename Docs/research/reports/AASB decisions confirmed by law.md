The confirmation round **upholds every structural fix from the first round** (ECL allowance, depreciation and
accrual mechanisms, Payday Super due dates, AASB 108 equity adjustments, Int 1031 gross cash flows) but changes
three defaults and sharpens four rules. GST on a prepayment is stricter than first reported: for a **single,
non-progressive supply the first part-payment attributes GST on the whole supply**, so the proportional Option B
is right only for Division 156 progressive or periodic supplies, which happen to cover most SMB services. Credit
notes in a later BAS period should default to the **Xero/QBO "negative sale" presentation** (G1 and 1A reduced
in the note's period), with the ATO-literal 1B form as a tenant setting rather than the default, because the net
GST payable is identical and every mainstream package works that way. Stripe clearing is a **receivable by
default, not by rule**, with a documented cash-equivalent override. Cancellable-contract netting of advance
invoices moves out of the MVP ledger into a **report-layer option**. The sharpened rules are a mandatory
reason code that forbids "credit loss" credit notes, a bad-debt write-off that needs a write-off record but no
adjustment note, Stripe input tax credits grouped by **UTC month** to match Stripe's monthly tax invoice, and
leave liabilities classified by **vesting** rather than expected settlement. Payday Super is now confirmed law
(Act No. 57 of 2025, assent 6 November 2025) and the ATO clearing house closed on 1 July 2026. LedgerForce's
design would put it **ahead of Xero, MYOB and QuickBooks** on credit losses, auto-reversals and prior-period
corrections, none of which those products handle natively. Twelve golden-posting changes go to the product
owner, listed at the end.

## GST law resolves four questions, two more strictly than first reported

### Q7 — GST on customer prepayments before a tax invoice (GP-012, GP-013)

| | |
|---|---|
| **(a) Rule** | On the accruals basis, s 29-5(1) attributes GST to the earlier of the period in which **any** consideration is received or an invoice is issued ([ES F2017L00425](https://legislation.gov.au/F2017L00425/asmade/2017-04-01/es/original/pdf); [GSTR 2000/29](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=GST%2FGSTR200029%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001&PiT=99991231235958)). A part payment under a contract is consideration when paid ([GSTR 2006/2](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf?DocId=GST%2FGSTR20062%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001&filename=law%2Fview%2Fpdf%2Fpbr%2Fgstr2006-002c2.pdf&PiT=20130701000001)). An ATO edited private ruling, persuasive only, says the first instalment "will trigger attribution for the total consideration" and that a Div 99 deposit must be "a genuine deposit and not a part payment" ([ATO EPR](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/sgif/ev/1011792288728-2.jpg)). Division 156 treats each progressive or periodic component as a separate supply, reported as each payment is received ([ATO progressive or periodic supplies](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/in-detail/rules-for-specific-transactions/agent-consignment-and-progressive-transactions/gst-agent-consignment-and-progressive-transactions/progressive-or-periodic-sales-and-purchases)). Commissioner's determinations limit attribution "to the extent" of consideration where the total is not known, and for retentions ([PAR 2017/3](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf/ldt/par2017-003.pdf); [PAR 2000/6](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf/ldt/par2000-006.pdf)); which instrument says what is **(unverified)**. |
| **(b) Software** | Xero's Receive Money "Prepayment" carries a tax rate on the amount received; its "Overpayment" carries none, and users report that it understates GST ([Xero product ideas](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967115-invoices-quotes/suggestions/47719241-customer-payments-payments-on-account-instead-o); [Poolwerx Xero guide](https://evolutionplatform.poolwerx.com.au/support/solutions/articles/13000109082-xero-record-a-prepayment)). No product models whole-supply attribution. MYOB and QBO AU deposit handling: no source found. |
| **(c) Recommendation** | A receipt before invoice for a taxable supply **always posts GST at receipt**. The receipt (or the deal it relates to) carries a supply-type flag. **Progressive/periodic (Div 156) or total not yet known → 1/11 of the receipt** (Option B), which is the usual SMB case (milestones, subscriptions, staged work). **Single non-progressive supply with known total → GST on the whole supply** at first receipt; the simplest product route is to require a full tax invoice at that point, so the ledger never carries GST ahead of its receivable. Untaxed prepayments only for GST-free or input-taxed supplies, flagged Div 99 security deposits (attributed when forfeited or applied) and unidentified money. The engine keeps a per-supply "GST attributed to date" so the final invoice never repeats it. |
| **(d) Change vs round 1** | **Sharpened.** Round 1 rejected Option A and adopted Option B with a whole-supply "caveat". This round makes the supply type a required input and Option B the Div 156 result, not a universal default. |
| **(e) Confidence** | **Medium-high.** Consistent across ATO binding rulings and private-ruling extracts. **Unverified:** s 29-5, Div 99, Div 156 text; GSTR 2000/35 paras 115 and 118; the "total consideration not known" instrument; MYOB and QBO behaviour. |
| **(f) Design / GP change** | Supersede **GP-012** with Option B labelled "Div 156 / total unknown"; add a single-supply variant (**new GP**) that issues a full tax invoice on first receipt; re-draft **GP-013** so the final period reports 1A +700.00 (see the change list); add `supply_type` and `div99_security_deposit` flags to prepayment receipts and deal templates; amend design §3.8 row 10. Accounting ADR (human gate). |

### Q8 — GST rounding default (GP-018, design §3.4)

| | |
|---|---|
| **(a) Rule** | For an invoice with several taxable sales the ATO allows the **total invoice rule** (total unrounded GST, round once, 0.5 cent up) or the **taxable supply rule** (GST per sale, rounded only where a line's GST has more decimals than the system stores, then summed and rounded), and "you and your customers don't need to use the same rounding rules" ([ATO tax invoices](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/tax-invoices)). ATO eInvoicing calculates GST by default under the total invoice rule ([ATO eInvoicing guide](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/23dcd77b380e47d4ba7e71d966c56502?v=ff6e9632)). Statutory root: s 9-90 ([EM 2000](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/print?DocID=NEM/EM200027/NAT/ATO/00008&PiT=99991231235958)). |
| **(b) Software** | QBO AU "follows the 'Taxable supply rule'" ([Intuit AU community](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-au/gst-and-bas/gst-rounding-2cents/01/1113739)). Xero computes GST per line to the cent, and users have asked it to "round GST on subtotal only" ([Xero idea](https://productideas.xero.com/forums/967115-invoices-quotes/suggestions/51245653-invoice-round-gst-on-subtotal-only-rather-than-li)). MYOB AccountRight uses a running-total method that nudges a line by a cent ([MYOB community](https://myob.lithium.com/discussions/sales_and_purchases/invoice-comes-through-0-01-out-due-to-gst-rounding-how-do-i-fix-in-myob/658401)). Reckon is per line ([Reckon community](https://community.reckon.com/discussion/comment/20616815/)). |
| **(c) Recommendation** | **Keep the total invoice rule as the AU default**, with the cent residual on the **largest revenue line** (ROUNDING stays zero on sales documents) and the printed invoice showing the same line nets the ledger posts. **Add a tenant option "per line, taxable supply rule, 2 dp"** for customers migrating from Xero/QBO or matching supplier documents; it is lawful when line GST is defined as stored to cents. Document expected 1-cent differences against Xero/QBO-issued invoices. |
| **(d) Change vs round 1** | **Default unchanged; option added.** Round 1 said GP-018a "matches neither rule" at 4 dp storage; this round finds per-line-to-cents lawful as a deliberate 2 dp setting, so 18a survives as an opt-in, not as the default. |
| **(e) Confidence** | **High** on legality; **medium** on vendor defaults (forum and community sources only). **Unverified:** s 9-90 text; whether the two rules sit in a legislative instrument or only in ATO guidance; MYOB Advanced "GST Rounding" document content. |
| **(f) Design / GP change** | Supersede **GP-018**: 18b becomes the default with the residual on the largest line (revenue 9.14, GST 0.91, no ROUNDING line); 18a retained as the opt-in `tax_rounding = per_line_2dp`. Amend design §3.4. Accounting ADR (human gate). |

### Q4 — Credit and adjustment notes on the BAS (GP-005, GP-019)

| | |
|---|---|
| **(a) Rule** | A price reduction or refund on an earlier sale is a **decreasing adjustment at 1B** (G18 on the worksheet), reported in the period you **become aware** of it; without the adjustment note at lodgement, it goes in the first BAS lodged after the note is received ([ATO how to make adjustments](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/making-adjustments-on-your-activity-statements/how-to-make-adjustments)). A valid adjustment note is needed unless the GST is **$75 or less** ([ATO adjustment notes](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/making-adjustments-on-your-activity-statements/adjustment-notes); [GSTR 2000/1](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/pdf?DocID=GST%2FGSTR20001%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001&filename=law%2Fview%2Fpdf%2Fpbr%2Fgstr2000-001c3.pdf&PiT=20101029000001)). That a same-period reduction simply nets into G1/1A (no adjustment arises) is **(unverified, prior knowledge of Div 19 / s 29-20)**. |
| **(b) Software** | Credit notes post as negative sales on the sales tax code dated on the note, reducing G1 and 1A in the note's period whatever the original invoice's period. Integrations report notes copy to Xero "as GST on Income" ([Axcelerate](https://support.axcelerate.com.au/hc/en-gb/community/posts/207549437/comments/210823707)); Zoho Books AU builds G1 from invoices and credit notes ([Zoho KB](https://zstatic.zohostatic.com/sa/books/kb/reports/gst-figures.md)). Changes in filed periods flow to Xero's next BAS as prior-period adjustments ([Flying Solo](https://www.flyingsolo.com.au/forums/topic/xero-correcting-gst-mistake/)). |
| **(c) Recommendation** | **Default: "software-compatible net"** — the note reduces G1 and 1A in its own period. **Tenant setting "ATO adjustment presentation"**: where the note relates to a sale attributed to an earlier lodged period, report its GST at 1B (G18) and leave G1 untouched. The PO may instead choose the strict form as default; tax payable is identical. Either way the supplier always issues a document titled as an adjustment note that references the original tax invoice (the $75 exemption is the recipient's, not the supplier's). A later-period debit note is an increasing adjustment at 1A. |
| **(d) Change vs round 1** | **Default reversed.** Round 1 proposed "same period → net; later period → 1B" as the rule. This round keeps that as the setting and makes the familiar net presentation the default; both are defensible. |
| **(e) Confidence** | **Medium.** **Unverified:** the same-period netting leg; the ATO G1 instructions on adjustments; Div 19, s 29-20 and s 29-75 text; no official Xero, MYOB or QBO help page read. |
| **(f) Design / GP change** | **GP-005** stays as drafted (same period) but gains a later-period variant showing both settings; **GP-019** later-period void follows the same setting; credit-note template meets adjustment-note content; tax code mapping becomes period-relative. Accounting ADR (human gate). |

### Q2 and Q3 — Stripe fee GST and bad-debt GST (GP-004, GP-007)

| | |
|---|---|
| **(a) Rule** | **Stripe:** an input tax credit is attributable only to a period for whose return the tax invoice is held at lodgement (s 29-10(3)) ([GSTR 2013/1](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GST%2FGSTR20131%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001)). Stripe charges GST on all AU fees and issues a **monthly** tax invoice, available by the 10th of the next month, none if tax details are missing ([Stripe GST AU](https://support.stripe.com/questions/goods-and-services-tax-gst-for-australia-based-businesses); [Stripe fee invoices](https://support.stripe.com/questions/download-tax-invoices-for-stripe-fees?locale=en-GB)), built from balance transactions by their **UTC "Created" timestamp** ([Stripe global taxation of fees](https://support.stripe.com/questions/global-taxation-of-stripe-fees)). **Bad debts:** on the accruals basis only, the supplier makes a decreasing adjustment of 1/11 of the amount written off (reduced for partly taxable sales) when the debt is written off or, if not, once 12 months overdue, at 1B; recovery is an increasing adjustment at 1A ([ATO types of adjustments](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/completing-your-bas-for-gst/making-adjustments-on-your-activity-statements/types-of-adjustments); [GSTR 2000/2](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?docid=GST%2FGSTR20002%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001)). |
| **(b) Software** | Xero and QBO users write off bad debts as a credit note coded to a bad-debts expense account ([Poolwerx Xero guide](https://evolutionplatform.poolwerx.com.au/support/solutions/articles/13000112168-managing-bad-debt); [QuickBooks community](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/global/service-items/write-off-bad-debt-in-quickbooks-online/00/383018)); one AU vendor warns that customer adjustments "will not reduce the GST collected on your BAS" without a dedicated bad-debt tax treatment ([Access Attaché](https://intercom.help/access-attache-83d6d2/en/articles/17422082)). Xero books Stripe through an unfed "Other" bank-type clearing account ([Synder](https://synder.com/blog/how-to-reconcile-stripe-payments-in-xero/)); no source on how any product gates Stripe 1B claims. |
| **(c) Recommendation** | **Stripe:** post fee and GST per payout (ledger unchanged); hold the 1B amount as **"pending tax invoice"** until Stripe's monthly invoice for that **UTC month** is attached; reconcile invoice GST to the sum of per-payout GST and post any cent difference (the invoice wins). Fees created before 10:00/11:00 AEST/AEDT on the 1st belong to the previous Stripe month, which matters for monthly lodgers. A late invoice is claimed in the first later BAS. Do not rely on the $75/$82.50 no-invoice exception. **Bad debts:** confirm GP-007's tax treatment (accruals 1B; cash basis no BAS effect; recovery 1A only for accruals tenants); require a **write-off record** (approver, date, amount) instead of an adjustment note; add a "12 months overdue" flag; make the GST adjustment a tax event separate from the AASB 9 write-off. |
| **(d) Change vs round 1** | **Refined.** Adds UTC-month grouping and invoice-total reconciliation (Stripe); settles "no adjustment note, but a write-off record" (bad debts). |
| **(e) Confidence** | **High** on the s 29-10(3) rule and the 1/11 adjustment; **medium** on Stripe mechanics (support-page extracts) and on the adjustment-note point (conflicting extracts, Div 21 text unread). **Unverified:** whether `balance_transaction.fee_details` exposes AU GST as a `tax` entry; whether Stripe's monthly document carries every s 29-70 element; the 4-year claim limit (s 93-5); recipient-side 12-month increasing adjustment; whether GP-004's 23.68 fee is GST-inclusive given Stripe adds GST on top. |
| **(f) Design / GP change** | **GP-004** gains an evidence status on the 1B line and a monthly reconciliation golden (**new GP**); confirm fee arithmetic against a real AU payout in T-0075. **GP-007** is superseded (see AASB 9 below); add a recovery golden and a recipient-side card. |

## AASB 9 and AASB 15 confirm the receivable fixes, with softer presentation

| | |

Practice Statement 2 is non-mandatory materiality guidance ([AASB PS 2](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/ps-2-dec-2022)). No regulator sets a default loss rate; illustrative SMB matrices run from under 1% current to about 48% over 90 days ([learnsignal](https://www.learnsignal.com/blog/ifrs-9-provision-matrix-trade-receivables/); [QAO](https://qao.qld.gov.au/blog/financial-instruments-changes-aasb-9-impairment)). |

Recovery: Dr bank / Cr impairment (or reinstate the receivable) plus a 1A increasing adjustment for accruals tenants. A one-click write-off with a proper journal, not a credit note that marks the invoice "paid", is a differentiator. |

### Credit-note reason codes (GP-005)

| | |
|---|---|
| **(a) Rule** | Price concessions, returns and variable-consideration adjustments reduce revenue (AASB 15 paras 47, 50–54); credit losses are impairment expense under AASB 9, a separate line (AASB 101 82(ba)). The IASB concedes IFRS 15 has no test separating an implicit price concession from impairment, that practice diverges, and in April 2024 decided on no further action ([IASB ap6a](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2024/april/iasb/ap6a-ifrs15-pir-applying-ifrs-15-with-ifrs-9.pdf); [iasplus April 2024](https://www.iasplus.com/en/meeting-notes/iasb/2024/april)). |
| **(b) Software** | Xero and QBO users make the distinction by account coding: bad-debt credit notes go to an expense account, others to revenue ([Poolwerx Xero guide](https://evolutionplatform.poolwerx.com.au/support/solutions/articles/13000112168-managing-bad-debt)). No product enforces it. |
| **(c) Recommendation** | Mandatory `reason_code` on every credit note: `price_adjustment`, `return`, `scope_reduction`, `billing_error`, `goodwill_concession` → reduce revenue (GP-005). **`credit_loss` is not a credit-note reason**; it routes to the write-off flow, because it is an AASB 9 derecognition and a Division 21 bad-debt adjustment, not a GST supply adjustment. Tenant guidance: commercial reasons are revenue reductions even for slow payers; credit loss only for inability to pay. Implicit concessions expected at inception are out of MVP scope. |
| **(d) Change vs round 1** | **Confirmed and tightened**: "credit loss" is blocked on credit notes rather than routed from one. |
| **(e) Confidence** | **High** (IASB 2024 paper). **Unverified:** conclusions of Illustrative Examples 2 and 3; no Australian SME guidance on reason coding. |
| **(f) Design / GP change** | **GP-005** adds `reason_code = scope_reduction` to its scenario; credit-note validation rule; no posting change. |

### Contract-liability, AR credit-balance and Stripe-clearing presentation

| | |
|---|---|
| **(a) Rule** | Prepayments and deferred revenue are contract liabilities (paras 105–106), and other captions are allowed (para 109, **unverified**) ([IFRS Community](https://ifrscommunity.com/knowledge-base/ifrs-15-contract-assets-and-contract-liabilities/)). Offsetting needs an enforceable right and intent to settle net (AASB 132 para 42), so one customer's credit cannot net another's debit ([MetroAG note](https://reports.metroag.de/annual-report/2019-2020/notes/notes-to-the-balance-sheet/38-offsetting-financial-assets-and-financial-liabilities.html)). Cash equivalents are short-term, highly liquid, readily convertible to known cash and of insignificant risk (AASB 107) ([AASB 107 PDF](https://www.aasb.gov.au/admin/file/content105/c9/AASB107_08-15.pdf)); AASB 107 does not name processor balances. A US filer treats card settlements under 5 days as cash equivalents ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/103595/000109690613001552/R23.htm)); that is a policy, not AASB authority. |

## Conclusion

This round shifts the open risk from "what does the law say" to "what does the primary text say exactly". Every
recommendation survived contact with a second set of sources, and the only reversals concern **defaults**, not
rules: where law permits two presentations (credit notes on the BAS, Stripe balances, per-line rounding), the
evidence favours shipping the familiar Xero/QBO behaviour as default and the stricter form as a setting, because
tax payable and profit are identical and migrating bookkeepers will otherwise read correct output as a bug. The
one place the law turned out stricter than the design assumed is the single-supply prepayment, and the cleanest
answer there is a workflow rule (issue the tax invoice) rather than a new kind of ledger entry.

The second implication is competitive. Mainstream AU software leaves ECL allowances, auto-reversing recurring
journals and prior-period corrections to manual workarounds, and several of its users' product-ideas requests
map one-for-one onto LedgerForce's proposed mechanisms. Building them in is therefore a compliance requirement and
a product advantage at the same time. Before any of the twelve changes is Approved, though, the same short list
of primary texts must be read once network access allows: s 29-5 and Div 156, s 9-90, Div 19 and Div 21, AASB 9
5.4.4 and B5.5.35, AASB 15 paras 105–109 and Example 38, the Payday Super Act's extended-period wording, the 2027
compilation of Interpretation 1031 and the final AASB 2026-X.
